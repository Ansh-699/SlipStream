### S3-01 · One permissionless call multiplies an uncapped funding premium by 48 uncapped catch-up intervals

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P0
- **Tag:** [reachable-now]
- **Class:** S3-C4
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:19-46`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:70-80`, `programs/slipstream/src/math/funding.rs:15-34`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/mod.rs:120`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:203-237`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/claim_funding.rs:99-104`

**What is wrong.** `compute_funding` takes the *instantaneous* premium
`(mark - index) / index` and multiplies it by `intervals`, the number of whole funding
intervals that elapsed since `last_funding_ts` — and neither factor has a bound. The
premium is an unclamped ratio (`funding.rs:23-26`); `intervals` is an unclamped
`elapsed / interval_secs` (`compute_funding.rs:41`). Every real perpetual clamps the
per-interval funding rate for exactly this reason, and the invariant is written into the
program's own units: `INTEREST_RATE_PER_INTERVAL` is documented at `funding.rs:5-7` as
"0.01% = 1 bps", the scale the index is meant to move at. There is no signer check
anywhere on the path — `mod.rs:120` dispatches straight into `process`, which requires
only that `market_acc` be program-owned (`compute_funding.rs:23-25`) — so any devnet
account holding tx-fee lamports can fire it. The mechanism that hid it: the one
regression test for this code (`tests/unit/src/test_compute_funding_regressions.rs:109`)
deliberately sets `twap_prices[0] = price` so mark == index and the premium is zero,
"to isolate interval scaling". The multiplier is therefore only ever exercised against a
rate of zero.

**Proof.** Live devnet market `ECUp8pXzVLzxjVs8mtKBJma3mdcHf8zSC4cqPeBy8MPy` read at
2026-08-21T19:13:46Z (snapshot and replay in `docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/`):

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/funding_catchup.py
=== one permissionless compute_funding call (disc 0x06) ===
  elapsed              1,401,026 s (16.22 days)
  intervals            48                <- UNCAPPED (compute_funding.rs:41)
  premium_rate         -189,297,877,255,452,698  (-18.929788% per interval)   <- UNCAPPED (funding.rs:23-26)
  rate_per_interval    -189,197,877,255,452,698  (-18.919788%)
  x 48 intervals      -9,081,498,108,261,729,504  (-908.1498% of notional)
  new cumulative index -9,120,169,729,644,858,361  (-9.120170)
```

Preconditions all hold on the live deployment today: `twap_count = 225` so
`get_twap()` is `Some` (`compute_funding.rs:51`); Switchboard's `result.value` is `0`,
so `oracle.rs:262-271` takes the Pyth-only fallback; the Pyth feed
`7UVimffxr9ow1uXYxsr4LHAcV58mLzhmwaeKvJ1pjLiE` published 44 s before the read, inside
the 60 s `MAX_STALENESS_SECS`, so `apply_dual_oracle` returns `Price(91_317_865)`.

The poisoned index becomes money through **`liquidate_position`**, not through
`claim_funding`. `claim_funding` never reads the oracle at all: it prices off
`market.mark_price_for_close(now_ts)` (`claim_funding.rs:61-63`), which returns `None` on
this deployment — `market.rs:173-182` rejects a stale mark and the mark is 16 days old
against `MARK_PRICE_MAX_STALENESS_MINS = 30` (`market.rs:16`) — so the instruction errors
`OracleStale` and pays nobody. That is S3-03 below, and it applies to `close_position` and
`execute_trigger` too. `liquidate_position` contains no `mark_price_for_close` call
anywhere in the file: it takes any signer (`liquidate_position.rs:57-59`), prices off
`apply_dual_oracle` (`:91-101`), folds the funding term into the health factor
(`:138-155`) and into `total_settlement` (`:203-206`), and settles that against
`UserAccount.free_collateral` or `insurance_fund_balance` (`:220-237`). Applied to the
actual 26 open positions:

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/live_positions.py
=== after one permissionless compute_funding call, via liquidate_position ===
  liquidatable BEFORE the call: 2 of 26
  liquidatable AFTER  the call: 11 of 26  (11 of the 14 shorts; the other 3 are over-collateralised enough to survive)
  their collateral, zeroed by :211 with no payout:  $   17,353.99
  settlement deficit beyond that collateral:        $   61,885.41
  insurance_fund_balance available to absorb it:    $       20.73
  => insurance fund drained to $0.00 (:230-231), and $61,864.69 is booked
     as protocol bad debt by the else-branch at :232-236 -- no ADL, no log.
  credited to free_collateral by :222-226 on this path: $0.00
```

The long side is *not* liquidatable at the poisoned index — `-funding_payment` inflates
its health factor, so `:157-163` returns `HealthFactorAboveThreshold` — which is why the
credited leg is latched rather than reachable-now (below).

**Blast radius.** Two consequences on two different clocks.

*Reachable today, no crank required.* One call poisons the cumulative funding index for
every position on the market. Through `liquidate_position` — permissionless,
oracle-priced, not gated on the dead crank — 11 of the 14 open shorts flip from healthy to
liquidatable in the same block. Liquidating them zeroes $17,353.99 of trader collateral
with no payout (`liquidate_position.rs:209-211`) and leaves a $61,885.41 settlement
deficit that the $20.73 `insurance_fund_balance` cannot absorb: the fund goes to zero at
`:230-231` and the remaining $61,864.69 is booked as protocol bad debt by the else-branch
at `:232-236`, with no ADL, no log, and no error. The attacker needs no privilege, no
position of their own to fire the instruction, and roughly 5,000 lamports.

*Latched until the crank resumes.* The other side of the poisoned index is a credit to
every long, minted with no counterparty (`claim_funding.rs:99-104`; that the debits are
capped while the credits are not is S3-05). It is not payable today — `claim_funding` and
`close_position` both go through `mark_price_for_close`, which is `None` (S3-03). A single
`crank_twap` call re-stamps `last_mark_price` and the minute (`crank_twap.rs:71-76`) and
unlatches it, and `crank_twap` is itself permissionless. Denominated at the
`last_mark_price` the market stores today ($74.114120) the long book's claim is
$58,696.99; if the crank restarts while Pyth still reads $91.317865 — `crank_twap.rs:72`
writes the fresh oracle price straight into `last_mark_price` — it is $72,322.04. Either
figure is then withdrawable with no vault-solvency check (`withdraw_collateral.rs:86`
gates only on `user.free_collateral < amount`) against a $655,915.85 vault whose insurance
fund the first consequence has already emptied.

The precondition (a large `intervals` count, or a large mark/index gap) is recreated by
any keeper outage, and K1 says outages happen.

**Suggested remediation.** Clamp the per-interval funding rate to a configured maximum
(e.g. ±75 bps) inside `compute_funding_rate`, and cap `intervals` at a small constant
(1–3) in `compute_funding` so a catch-up can never apply more than a bounded multiple of
that clamp in one call. Both bounds belong in the program, not in the keeper, because the
instruction is permissionless.

### S3-02 · `compute_funding` prices the mark off a raw `get_twap()`, the one price read `mark_price_for_close` does not cover

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P1
- **Tag:** [reachable-now]
- **Class:** S3-C13
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:48-52`, `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:163-183`, `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:12-16`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/settle_trades.rs:235`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/settle_trades.rs:275`

**What is wrong.** `Market::mark_price_for_close` exists precisely so that a dead crank
cannot silently price a settlement, and all three of its callers use it
(`close_position.rs:123-125`, `claim_funding.rs:61-63`, `execute_trigger.rs:81-83`).
`compute_funding` is a fourth price consumer and it bypasses the gate entirely: it calls
`market.get_twap()` directly (`compute_funding.rs:51`), which returns the mean of the
`twap_prices` ring with no timestamp attached and no freshness test of any kind. The
funding index it writes is then consumed by all three gated callers, so the gate protects
the *settlement* price while leaving the *rate* it settles against sourced from a
16-day-old book. Secondary, in the same gate: the doc comment at `market.rs:12-16`
justifies the generous 30-minute window with "every settled fill also refreshes it" —
that refresh path does not exist. `settle_trades.rs:275` only *reads* `last_mark_price`,
and `settle_trades.rs:235` explicitly forbids sourcing it from a fill price. `crank_twap`
is the only writer, so the real budget is 225 missed cranks from one process, not two
independent refreshers.

**Proof.** The gate itself is sound and is firing correctly today — that is the negative
result that makes the asymmetry legible:

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/funding_catchup.py
=== Market::is_mark_price_fresh (state/market.rs:154-161) ===
  now_min 35649  stamp 12436  wrapping_sub 23213 min (16.1 days)  fresh=False
  => mark_price_for_close() returns None (state/market.rs:173-183)
  last_mark_price                    74,114,120  ($74.114120)
  get_twap()                         74,031,587  ($74.031587)
  pyth index price                   91,317,865  ($91.317865)
```

So on the same account, in the same block: the three gated callers refuse to run, while
`compute_funding` reads `$74.031587` as a live mark and prices it against a `$91.317865`
index — a -18.93% premium that is an artefact of the outage, not of the market. A grep of
every `last_mark_price` / `set_mark_price_minute` write in the program confirms the single
writer and confirms no path can leave the price set with the stamp cleared:

```
$ grep -rn "last_mark_price *=\|set_mark_price_minute" --include='*.rs' programs/
programs/slipstream/src/instructions/initialize_market.rs:178:    market.last_mark_price = 0;
programs/slipstream/src/instructions/crank_twap.rs:72:    market.last_mark_price = price;
programs/slipstream/src/instructions/crank_twap.rs:76:    market.set_mark_price_minute(now_min);
```

**Blast radius.** Everyone holding a position when a crank outage ends. This is the
delivery mechanism for S3-01's magnitude: without the un-gated TWAP read the premium
would be a normal basis, and the 48× multiplier would move the index by tens of basis
points instead of 908%. It also mis-prices funding continuously in the ordinary case,
because the TWAP is a 30-minute lagging average of the *local book* midprice while the
index is a spot oracle read.

**Suggested remediation.** Route `compute_funding`'s mark through
`mark_price_for_close(now)` (or an equivalent freshness-stamped read) and return
`OracleStale` when it is `None`, so funding cannot accrue against a dead feed. Separately,
correct the `market.rs:12-16` comment to name `crank_twap` as the only refresher, since
the staleness budget was sized on the belief that there were two.

### S3-03 · The stale-mark gate is one-sided: liquidation runs off the live oracle while the owner's exit paths are hard-blocked

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P1
- **Tag:** [reachable-now]
- **Class:** S3-C6
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:89-101`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:126-163`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:119-125`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/claim_funding.rs:59-63`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/execute_trigger.rs:78-86`, `PRODUCT.md:51`

**What is wrong.** The health factor that authorises a liquidation is computed from
`apply_dual_oracle`'s **live** reading (`liquidate_position.rs:91-101`, feeding
`compute_notional`/`compute_unrealized_pnl`/`compute_health_factor` at lines 132-155).
Every path by which the *owner* can act on the same position — `close_position`,
`claim_funding`, `execute_trigger`'s stop-loss — is gated on `mark_price_for_close`, which
is `None` whenever the crank is dead. The two prices are therefore allowed to diverge
without limit, and the divergence is one-directional: the protocol can seize a position at
a price the owner is forbidden from transacting at, including a stop-loss the owner placed
in advance for exactly this scenario. No document states this asymmetry; `PRODUCT.md:51`
lists "partial close and slippage-bounded close-at-market" and "keeper-executed stop-loss
/ take-profit triggers" as *live capabilities*, and both are hard-down on the deployment
today.

**Proof.** Both halves verified against the same live account snapshot:

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/live_positions.py
=== liquidate_position health at the live oracle ($91.317865) ===
  8hgAVi6dWYZjFGnokfBU4prJGAxRhrVSaBfUuAM4M64f  size   -0.600 SOL  collateral $    5.71  uPnL $    -5.83  health 0.947
  DrVL7kqFS5kSqN54v3Ris94omj6uf3zi1mv3NFNvFSwK  size   -0.400 SOL  collateral $   20.00  uPnL $   -36.13  health 0.000
  => 2 of 26 positions are liquidatable NOW.
     Their owners cannot call close_position at all: mark_price_for_close()
     returns None (state/market.rs:173-183) so it errors OracleStale.
```

`liquidate_position` has no dependency on `mark_price_for_close` anywhere in its 363
lines, and its remaining gates are all open on the live market: `circuit_breaker_active`
is `0`, `restricted_mode` is `0`, and the Pyth-only fallback at `oracle.rs:262-271`
returns `Price(..)` because Switchboard's `result.value` is `0` and Pyth is 44 s fresh.

**Blast radius.** The two positions above today, and every position during any future
crank outage. A trader who did everything right — sized conservatively, placed a stop —
is liquidated at the oracle price while their own stop-loss returns `OracleStale`, and
they eat the loss plus S3-04's uncollected bounty deduction. On mainnet this is the
classic "exchange halts the exit but not the liquidator" failure; it converts a keeper
outage into realised trader losses.

**Suggested remediation.** Make the two sides share one price source. Either allow the
owner-initiated close paths to fall back to the same `apply_dual_oracle` reading
`liquidate_position` already uses (passing the feed accounts in), or gate
`liquidate_position` on mark freshness too so that a dead crank freezes both directions.
Freezing both is the safer default; freezing only the exit is the one combination that
should not ship.

### S3-04 · The liquidator bounty is deducted from the trader and paid to nobody

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P1
- **Tag:** [reachable-now]
- **Class:** S3-C8
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:187-193`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:203-237`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:337-362`, `tests/unit/src/test_liquidate_position_regressions.rs:58`, `PRODUCT.md:51`

**What is wrong.** Lines 187-193 compute `liquidator_bonus` and the comment states the
invariant: "Compute liquidator bonus: 50bps of notional, capped at 20% of the position's
remaining net collateral, per §16." The value is then used on exactly one further line —
`- (liquidator_bonus as i128)` at line 206 — which reduces `total_settlement`, i.e.
reduces what the liquidated trader is credited at line 222-226. Nothing ever credits the
liquidator with it, and nothing adds it to `insurance_fund_balance` either (the `else`
branch at 227-237 only *subtracts* from the fund). The liquidator's entire compensation is
the `LiquidationIntent` rent moved at lines 356-361, which only exists on the
`pending_fills > 0` path and is a few thousand lamports of SOL, not a fee proportional to
the risk taken. So the bounty is *charged* and *destroyed*: the USDC stays in the vault
with no `free_collateral` claim against it.

**Proof.** Every reference to the value in the entire program:

```
$ grep -rn "liquidator_bonus" --include='*.rs' programs/ tests/
programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:193:    let liquidator_bonus = bonus_bps.min(bonus_pct);
programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:206:        - (liquidator_bonus as i128);
```

Two references, neither of which pays anybody. Worked example replayed from the program's
own arithmetic:

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/funding_catchup.py
=== liquidator bonus (liquidate_position.rs:187-237) ===
  notional $900.00  50bps=$4.50  20% of net=$1.00  -> bonus $1.00
  trader credited $4.00 (bonus deducted, line 206)
  liquidator credited: $0.00 -- `liquidator_bonus` is referenced on exactly
  two lines of the file (193, 206) and neither pays anybody.
```

The mechanism that hid it: the two Mollusk tests over this instruction
(`tests/unit/src/test_liquidate_position_regressions.rs:58` and `:185`) cover a forged
intent account and the healthy-position early return; neither reaches the settlement
branch, and no test in the repo asserts on a liquidator's balance. The only prose
describing the payout lives in `tests/integration/`, which K8 records as never run.

**Blast radius.** Two distinct harms. (1) Every liquidated trader is over-charged by the
bonus amount — up to 20% of their remaining net collateral, since `bonus_pct` at line 192
is `net_collateral / 5` and is usually the binding cap on an underwater position. (2) The
liquidation incentive does not exist, and it degrades exactly where it matters: when
`net_collateral` reaches 0 the cap makes the bonus 0 as well, so nobody is paid to clear
the positions that generate bad debt, and the $20.73 insurance fund absorbs it. On mainnet
this means liquidations only happen while the operator's own keeper is alive — which K1
shows is not a safe assumption.

**Suggested remediation.** Credit `liquidator_bonus` to the liquidator's `UserAccount`
(add the account to the instruction and `checked_add` it into `free_collateral`) in the
same branch that deducts it, and add a floor so the bounty stays non-zero on an underwater
position — funded from the insurance fund rather than from collateral that is already
gone. Add a Mollusk test that asserts the liquidator's balance changed.

### S3-05 · `claim_funding` forgives an uncollectable funding debit while crediting the other side unconditionally

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P1
- **Tag:** [mainnet-only]
- **Class:** S3-C3
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/claim_funding.rs:88-104`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:232-252`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/withdraw_collateral.rs:86`

**What is wrong.** The paying side's debit is applied with `saturating_sub`
(`claim_funding.rs:97`): if `free_collateral` plus the position's collateral is less than
what is owed, the shortfall is silently dropped — no `insurance_fund_balance` debit, no
log, no error, and the position's funding snapshot has already been advanced at line 75,
so the debt cannot be re-collected later. The receiving side's credit at lines 100-103 is
unconditional `checked_add` into `free_collateral` with no counterparty and no source
account. Funding is therefore not conservative: the credits are minted, the debits are
capped, and the difference is a claim on the shared vault. The program itself shows it
knows this matters — the equivalent deficit in `close_position.rs:232-252` is debited from
the insurance fund *and* logged with `sol_log_64` because "a silent one is unauditable".
`claim_funding` does neither. `withdraw_collateral.rs:86` gates a withdrawal only on
`user.free_collateral < amount`, so a minted credit is directly withdrawable.

**Proof.** Every write to a balance in `claim_funding.rs`, showing there is no third
account and no fund debit anywhere in the file:

```
$ grep -n "free_collateral\|collateral =\|insurance" programs/slipstream/src/instructions/claim_funding.rs
90:        if user_mut.free_collateral >= abs_payment {
91:            user_mut.free_collateral -= abs_payment;
94:            let shortfall = abs_payment - user_mut.free_collateral;
95:            user_mut.free_collateral = 0;
97:            pos_mut2.collateral = pos_mut2.collateral.saturating_sub(shortfall);
100:        user_mut.free_collateral = user_mut
101:            .free_collateral
102:            .checked_add((-payment) as u64)
```

Replayed against the live position set, using the index S3-01 can install today:

```
$ python3 docs/audit/audit-e2e/repro/s3/live_positions.py
  SHORT side  collateral $   29,705.36   OWES        $   78,013.17
  collectable from shorts (capped by their collateral):  $   29,705.36
  SILENTLY FORGIVEN by saturating_sub, claim_funding.rs:97: $ 48,307.81
```

Tagged `[mainnet-only]` because `claim_funding` currently reverts before reaching this
code — its own `mark_price_for_close` gate at line 61-63 returns `OracleStale` (S3-03). It
becomes reachable on devnet the moment a single `crank_twap` runs, which is a keeper
restart away.

**Blast radius.** Every depositor in the shared quote vault. In the ordinary case the leak
is small and one-sided (an underwater payer's residue), but it is unbounded in principle
because nothing ties the credited total to the collected total, and S3-01 shows the
magnitude a single call can reach. The cost lands on whoever withdraws last.

**Suggested remediation.** Make the funding transfer conservative: debit the shortfall
from `insurance_fund_balance` (and log it, as `close_position` does) when the payer cannot
cover, and refuse to credit the receiving side beyond what the market can actually source.
A per-market funding pool that credits are drawn from is the structural fix; the insurance
debit plus a log is the minimum.

### S3-06 · `cancel_trigger` and `execute_trigger` act on accounts they never bind

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P2
- **Class:** S3-C10
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/cancel_trigger.rs:30-35`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/cancel_trigger.rs:40-53`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/execute_trigger.rs:74-86`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:321-349`

**What is wrong.** Two missing bindings, both currently constrained by something else.
(1) `cancel_trigger` loads the trigger with `TriggerOrder::from_account_info`, which checks
only `data[0] == DISC_TRIGGER_ORDER` (`trigger_order.rs:38-47`), then compares
`trigger.owner` to the signer and hands the account to `close_trigger_account`, which
zeroes all of its data and drains all of its lamports. It never checks
`trigger_acc.owner() == program_id` and never re-derives the
`["trigger", owner, market_index, kind]` PDA — unlike `execute_trigger.rs:51-59`, which
does re-derive, and unlike `close_liquidation_intent`, whose 25-line comment block at
`liquidate_position.rs:321-336` exists specifically because that same pair of checks was
missing there and was added. The sibling close path was hardened; this one was not.
(2) `execute_trigger` evaluates the whole trigger condition — `mark_price_for_close` at
line 82 and `is_met` at line 84 — against a `market_acc` whose owner and PDA have not been
checked; that validation happens later, inside `do_close` (`close_position.rs:83-93`).

perform them:

### S3-08 · Three unguarded numeric edges in the shared math (batched)

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P3
- **Class:** S3-C2
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/math/fixed_point.rs:108-113`, `programs/slipstream/src/math/fixed_point.rs:123-125`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:178-183`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:196-201`, `programs/slipstream/src/math/funding.rs:79`

### S3-09 · A short `close_position` payload is silently reinterpreted as an unbounded full close

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P3
- **Class:** S3-C11
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:47-54`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:127-138`, `client/src/instructions.ts:816-825`

settlement uses at line 154), and `0` disabling it is documented at lines 22-27, so the

the program delivers neither without saying so.

**Proof.** The in-tree SDK only ever emits the 1-byte or 17-byte form, which is why
nothing has hit this:

### S3-10 · A permissionless crank inside the 1-in-45-days `stamp == 0` minute switches the stale-mark gate off forever

- **Status:** CONFIRMED
- **Severity:** P1
- **Tag:** [reachable-now]
- **Class:** S3-C13
- **Evidence:** `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/crank_twap.rs:75-76`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/crank_twap.rs:19-29`, `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:156-158`, `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:139-140`, `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:173-182`, `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:119-125`

`let now_min = ((now_ts / 60) as u64 % 65536) as u16;` and stamps it, so `now_min` is `0`

writes a non-zero oracle price (guarded non-zero at `:49-51`) and line `:76` writes a stamp
of `0`. From then on `mark_price_for_close` (`market.rs:173-182`) serves that frozen price

and `market.rs:139-140`, which names this very case ("the ~1-in-45-days minute that hashes

also lands there. `crank_twap` is permissionless — `crank_twap.rs:19-29` destructures the
accounts and checks only `market_acc.owner()`, with no `is_signer` test anywhere in the
file — so an attacker chooses the window rather than waiting for a keeper to hit it.

**Proof.** Replay of the two functions' exact integer arithmetic against the live
`last_mark_price` from the same devnet snapshot the other S3 repros use

The third row is the harmful direction: a position whose margin has been eroded by funding
or a partial close is already liquidatable (its crossing price is above the entry) while
the table still shows a comfortable $97.50. The UI number does not move with collateral at
all, so it cannot show this.

**Blast radius.** Every user sizing a position against the displayed number. On the live
deployment the error compounds with S3-03: the table says you are safe, the program
liquidates you, and your close button returns `OracleStale`.

**Suggested remediation.** Compute the crossing from the same inputs the program uses —
stored collateral, notional at the current mark, and accrued funding — and if collateral
from legacy ER fills is genuinely untrustworthy, render "—" for those positions rather
than a confident wrong number.

## Cleared

- **S3-C1** — Rounding direction. Every division in the slice is Rust integer division,
  i.e. truncation toward zero. One of them is **not** bounded at 1 atom and is not cleared
  here: `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:41`
  (`let intervals = elapsed / interval_secs`) truncates by up to one whole funding
  interval, not one atom. The truncation itself loses nothing — `next_funding_ts` advances
  by `intervals * interval_secs` rather than to `now`, so the remainder stays owed
  (`programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:42-45`) — but the quotient it
  produces is the uncapped catch-up multiplier `S3-01` files
  (`programs/slipstream/src/instructions/compute_funding.rs:19-46`), and the divisor is
  never validated non-zero, which is `S3-07`. The remaining divisions are all bounded at
  1 atom of error:
  `programs/slipstream/src/math/fixed_point.rs:24` (`mul_price`, floor),
  `:40` (`div_price`, floor), `:57` (`compute_notional`, floor -> notional understated ->
  margin and fee understated, favours the user), `:69` (`compute_initial_margin`, floor,
  favours the user), `:74` (`compute_maintenance_margin`, floor -> health overstated,
  liquidation delayed, favours the user), `:82` (`apply_bps`, floor -> fee and liquidator
  bonus understated), `:133` (`compute_health_factor`, floor -> health understated,
  favours the protocol), `:159` (`compute_vwap_entry`, floor),
  `programs/slipstream/src/math/funding.rs:90` (`compute_twap`, floor),
  `programs/slipstream/src/state/market.rs:133` (`get_twap`, floor),
  `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/close_position.rs:174` (proportional collateral
  release, floor -> the remainder stays in the position, neutral), and
  `programs/slipstream/src/instructions/liquidate_position.rs:192` (`net_collateral / 5`,
  floor). Three of these divide a value that can be **negative** —
  `programs/slipstream/src/math/fixed_point.rs:111` (PnL),
  `programs/slipstream/src/math/funding.rs:26` (premium) and
  `programs/slipstream/src/math/funding.rs:77` (payment) — where truncation toward zero is
  *asymmetric across the sign*: it always shrinks the magnitude, so a payer pays 1 atom
  less and a receiver receives 1 atom less. No round trip in the slice compounds this
  (each is a single division on an independent quantity), and 1 atom is $0.000001, so this
  is not a value leak. Recorded as the enumeration this class asks for, not as a finding.

- **S3-C4** — i128 funding-index packing. Covered by S3-01. The lo/hi split itself is
